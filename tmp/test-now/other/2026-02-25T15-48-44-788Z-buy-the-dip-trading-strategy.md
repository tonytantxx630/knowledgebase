## Key points
- Buy the dip is a mean reversion strategy: buying assets after a price decline toward a support level or historical average
- Define 'dip' objectively: specific percentage drop (e.g., 5-10%), horizontal support, or moving average touch
- Works best in strong uptrends - buying the dip in a dying trend leads to catching falling knives
- Always use a stop loss below the entry - never average down blindly without a defined exit
- Position sizing is critical: reserve capital for multiple entries if the dip deepens
- Wait for confirmation: bounce off support, bullish candlestick patterns, or volume spike before entering
- Risk-reward ratio must justify the trade: target at least 2:1 reward relative to stop loss distance
- Not all dips are buyable: distinguish between normal pullbacks and trend reversals
- Dollar cost averaging (DCA) is a systematic approach to buying dips over time
- Emotional discipline required: avoid FOMO entries at local tops or panic selling at bottoms
- Backtest the specific dip-buying parameters on the asset/timeframe before committing capital
- Combine with trend analysis - only buy dips in assets with overall bullish structure
