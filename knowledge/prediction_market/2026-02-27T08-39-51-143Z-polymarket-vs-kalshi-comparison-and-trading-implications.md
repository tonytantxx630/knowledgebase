## Key points
- Fee structure directly impacts arbitrage viability - always calculate all-in costs including maker/taker fees before entering arbitrage trades
- Poor liquidity creates a vicious cycle: less liquidity leads to worse pricing, which causes user exodus, further reducing liquidity
- Polymarket uses maker rebates to incentivize liquidity provision - becoming a maker (providing liquidity) earns rebates rather than paying taker fees
- Trade larger markets with better liquidity to ensure reliable order execution and minimize slippage
- Monitor real-time price differences between Polymarket and Kalshi for cross-platform arbitrage opportunities
- Maker rebates can offset trading costs significantly for high-volume liquidity providers
- Fee changes by platforms can rapidly eliminate existing arbitrage margins without warning
- Regulatory distinction matters: Kalshi is US-regulated while Polymarket is not, affecting counterparty risk assessments
- Professional traders withdraw from markets when fees erode arbitrage opportunities beyond viable thresholds
- Cross-platform arbitrage requires careful consideration of withdrawal times and counterparty settlement risk
