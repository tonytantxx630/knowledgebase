## Key points
- Binary outcomes resolve to $1.00 (winner) or $0.00 (loser); prices represent implied probability from 0-100%
- Information edge strategy: research events better than market consensus before price adjusts; domain expertise provides sustainable advantage
- Market making: provide liquidity on both sides of outcome to capture spread plus liquidity rewards; requires capital and inventory management
- Arbitrage: exploit pricing inefficiencies across correlated markets or against external event probabilities; execution speed critical for profitability
- Zero trading fees (as of 2026) with low Polygon gas ($0.001-$0.01) enables frequent position adjustments; batch transactions to minimize costs
- Resolution risk: understand exact criteria for outcome determination; avoid ambiguous markets and check historical resolution patterns
- Oracle mechanics: UMA optimistic oracle handles resolution; monitor dispute activity and understand dispute mechanism before trading
- Position sizing: never risk more than acceptable loss; binary outcomes mean total position loss possible with no partial wins
- Liquidity consideration: wider spreads in illiquid markets increase entry/exit costs; favor high-volume markets for better fills
- Manipulation risk: thinly traded markets easily moved by large traders; monitor whale activity and avoid low-volume event markets
- Stop-loss usage: set automatic exit points to protect against adverse price moves and maintain emotional discipline
- Platform risk: smart contract vulnerabilities and regulatory uncertainty exist; assess risk tolerance accordingly
- Alternative platforms: consider Kalshi (CFTC-regulated), Manifold Markets, or Betfair based on regulatory status, liquidity, and geographic restrictions
