## Key points
- Major Fibonacci retracement levels (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) identify potential support/resistance zones during price pullbacks within trends.
- The 61.8% level (golden ratio inverse) is the most critical decision point; 78.6% represents the final retracement leg before potential trend failure.
- Implementation requires: (1) confirm trend direction, (2) draw levels from swing low to high, (3) wait for price to reach level, (4) confirm with candlestick patterns/RSI/volume, (5) enter with stop loss.
- Fibonacci works best with confluence: layer with candlestick patterns, trendlines, moving averages, and volume to validate entries and reduce false signals.
- Fibonacci extensions (127.2%, 150%, 161.8%, 261.8%) provide data-driven profit targets beyond retracement levels; 161.8% is most popular for swing trading exits.
- Subjectivity risk: Different traders may identify different swing points, leading to inconsistent level placement and conflicting signals.
- Fibonacci levels have no predictive power alone—price can blow through levels in volatile or news-driven markets without reversing.
- Self-fulfilling prophecy effect: Levels may work because traders and algorithms collectively monitor them, creating clustering of orders.
- Requires trending environment: Applying Fibonacci in sideways/choppy markets yields misleading setups; must confirm trend first.
- Fibonacci does not account for timing, volume, or volatility—price may consolidate at levels for extended periods or reverse due to external catalysts.
- Best practice: Wait for price pullbacks during trends rather than chasing moves; set stops just beyond next retracement level to manage risk.
- Risk-reward optimization: Scale out at multiple extension levels to lock in profits progressively while letting winners run.
- Never use Fibonacci in isolation—combine with trend confirmation, market context, volume analysis, and proper position sizing for comprehensive system.
