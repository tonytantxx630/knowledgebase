## Key points
- Gold traditionally serves as a hedge against macroeconomic uncertainty, inflation, and currency devaluation
- Always use stop-loss orders when holding gold positions to limit downside risk
- Gold's hedging properties are most effective during periods of monetary policy uncertainty or geopolitical stress
- Position sizing for gold should account for its volatility - smaller positions allow for wider stops
- The hedge narrative can reverse during periods of strong real yields, reducing gold's protective value
- Stop placement for gold should consider relevant support levels and market microstructure
- Combining gold with stop discipline creates a risk-defined macro hedge position
- Exit stops can be trailed upward as gold positions become profitable to lock in gains
