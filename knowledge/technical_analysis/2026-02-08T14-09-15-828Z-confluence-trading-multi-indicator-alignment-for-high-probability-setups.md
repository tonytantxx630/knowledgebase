## Key points
- Confluence trading filters noise by requiring multiple independent technical factors to align before entering trades
- Confluence zones form where support/resistance levels, moving averages, and trend lines converge on the chart
- Combine trend direction (moving averages) with momentum indicators (RSI, MACD) to confirm strength and timing
- Validate setups with candlestick patterns (hammers, engulfing) occurring at confluence zones for higher probability entries
- Multi-timeframe alignment: only trade lower timeframe signals (15-min) when aligned with higher timeframe trend (4-hour)
- Entry checklist requires three conditions: price at support + bullish candlestick pattern + RSI crossing out of oversold
- Exit at predefined resistance levels or when bearish divergence appears on MACD/RSI indicators
- Risk management: limit exposure to 1-2% of capital per trade with stops placed just beyond confluence structure
- More confluence factors increase reliability but may reduce trade frequency—balance confirmation with opportunity cost
- Confluence approach prevents impulsive entries by requiring objective multi-factor validation before execution
