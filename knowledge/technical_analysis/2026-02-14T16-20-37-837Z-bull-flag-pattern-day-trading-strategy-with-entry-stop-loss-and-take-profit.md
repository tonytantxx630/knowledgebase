## Key points
- Bull flag is a bullish continuation pattern: strong upward move (flagpole) followed by short downward/sideways consolidation (flag).
- Three confirmations required before entry: (1) valid pattern structure (uptrend → flagpole → consolidation), (2) strong breakout candle closing decisively above flag resistance, (3) high volume on breakout relative to recent average.
- Entry point: enter at candle close after breakout confirmation; avoid mid-candle entries to reduce false breakout risk.
- Stop loss placement (conservative): below the low of the entire bull flag pattern for maximum protection.
- Stop loss placement (aggressive): below a clear recent swing low inside the flag for tighter risk management.
- Take profit target: minimum 1.5:1 risk-reward ratio, or use measured move (project flagpole height from breakout point).
- Higher timeframe trend context is critical: pattern reliability increases when aligned with dominant trend.
- Volume confirmation validates breakout: breakout candle should have significantly higher volume than recent average.
- Common mistake: entering without all three confirmations leads to false breakouts and losses.
- Common mistake: stops placed too tight near breakout level get hit by normal volatility before move continues.
- Common mistake: chasing the pattern after initial breakout; either take first entry or wait for retest of breakout level.
- Pattern structure must be valid: requires clear uptrend context before flagpole forms, and flag consolidation should be relatively short compared to flagpole.
- Pre-trade planning essential: define entry price, stop loss, and take profit levels before entering position.
