## Key points
- RSI is a 0-100 momentum indicator measuring price speed and direction; effectiveness depends on correct market regime identification
- In trending markets, use RSI for continuation signals—uptrends keep RSI above 50 (often above 70), so selling overbought levels fights the trend
- In ranging markets, use RSI for mean reversion—RSI above 70-80 suggests range top, below 20-30 suggests range bottom
- The RSI 50 midline acts as a bias filter: uptrends hold above 50 with dips below 40 signaling weakness; downtrends hold below 50 with spikes above 60 signaling weakness
- Bullish regime: RSI oscillates between 40-80 making higher floors; bearish regime: RSI oscillates between 20-60 making lower ceilings
- Regular bullish divergence: price makes lower low while RSI makes higher low, signals potential reversal up
- Regular bearish divergence: price makes higher high while RSI makes lower high, signals potential reversal down
- Hidden bullish divergence: price makes higher low while RSI makes lower low, signals uptrend continuation
- Hidden bearish divergence: price makes lower high while RSI makes higher high, signals downtrend continuation
- RSI period settings: 7-9 for day trading, 14-21 for swing trading
- RSI identifies favorable conditions but requires price action confirmation (candlestick patterns) for entry triggers
- Avoid the overbought-equals-sell trap: in strong uptrends, RSI can remain overbought for extended periods
