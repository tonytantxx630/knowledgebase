## Key points
- Squeeze pattern occurs when Bollinger Bands tighten significantly, signaling reduced volatility and consolidation that often precedes sharp price movements
- Bollinger BandWidth (BBW) formula: ((Upper Band − Lower Band) / Middle Band) × 100; track over 8-12 months to identify historically low levels indicating squeeze
- Entry signal: wait for strong breakout candle closing well beyond Bollinger Band (20-period SMA ± 2 standard deviations), not just slight breach
- Confirm breakouts with multiple factors: increased volume, alignment with support/resistance levels, and complementary indicators (RSI, MACD, volume analysis)
- ATR-based stop-loss placement: use 2.0× multiplier for normal volatility, 2.5×-3.0× for high volatility; initial stop just below opposite Bollinger Band
- Chandelier Exit method: for longs, stop at highest high since entry minus (ATR × multiplier); for shorts, lowest low plus (ATR × multiplier)
- When price moves favorably by ~2× ATR, tighten stop-loss to lock profits while allowing room for continued movement
- Position sizing strategy: start smaller during squeeze phase, increase only after confirming breakout direction; adjust size based on current BandWidth
- Multi-timeframe validation: confirm squeeze patterns across multiple timeframes to strengthen signal reliability
- False breakout filters: watch for volume-price divergences, momentum indicator misalignment, and failed tests at support/resistance levels
- Strategy performs best during transitions from low to high volatility; struggles in persistently volatile or range-bound markets
- Volume profile: decreasing volume during squeeze suggests authentic consolidation; breakout volume should increase significantly
- Before major economic events, widen stop-loss settings to accommodate increased volatility spikes
- Risk management formula: Position Size = (Amount at risk) / (ATR-based stop distance); diversify across uncorrelated markets
