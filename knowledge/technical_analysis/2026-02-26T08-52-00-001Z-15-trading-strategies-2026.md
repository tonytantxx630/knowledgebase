## Key points
- Trading strategies must match current market conditions: bull markets favor buying on dips, bear markets require risk management and hedging, sideways markets suit range trading and mean reversion.
- Trend trading focuses on identifying and riding market trends using moving averages, MACD, and trendlines - profits from sustained market direction.
- Range trading exploits repetitive price bounces between support and resistance levels using RSI or Stochastic Oscillator in sideways markets.
- Breakout trading captures early gains when price breaks key levels with volume confirmation - works in trending or volatile markets.
- Momentum trading rides market momentum until it loses strength using RSI, MACD, and volume indicators.
- Mean reversion profits from prices returning to historical average using moving averages, Bollinger Bands, and RSI.
- Fibonacci retracement identifies high-probability reversal points at key Fibonacci levels (38.2%, 50%, 61.8%) during corrections.
- Swing trading captures multi-day swings in price momentum, holding positions for days or weeks.
- Day trading involves buying and selling within a single trading day to capitalize on short-term fluctuations.
- Scalping generates frequent small profits through rapid trades, holding positions for seconds to minutes.
- Position trading benefits from large long-term moves with minimal trading using fundamental analysis and weekly/monthly charts.
- Price action trading uses candlestick patterns, trendlines, and support/resistance without heavy indicators.
- Gap trading profits from market adjustments after large overnight price gaps using pre-market data and volume spikes.
- End-of-day trading uses daily close data for informed decisions without constant monitoring.
- Algorithmic trading executes trades at high speed with precision using predefined rules and backtesting.
