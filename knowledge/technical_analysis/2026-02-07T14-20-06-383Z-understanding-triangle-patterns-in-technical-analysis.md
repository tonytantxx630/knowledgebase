## Key points
- Triangles are continuation patterns that narrow from widest point to apex, showing decreasing interest from both buyers and sellers as supply and demand converge.
- Ascending triangles (bullish) form with flat resistance at top and rising support below; typically signal continuation of uptrend when broken with volume.
- Descending triangles (bearish) form with flat support at bottom and declining resistance above; typically signal continuation of downtrend when broken with volume.
- Symmetrical triangles form with converging trendlines showing indecision; breakout direction typically follows existing trend and requires volume confirmation.
- Volume is critical: should decrease during formation (indecision) and increase significantly at breakout (confirmation). Low volume breakouts are often false signals.
- Price targets are measured by taking the widest section of the triangle and projecting that distance from the breakout point.
- Typical formation time is around 4 weeks; patterns rarely last more than 90 days before resolving.
- Confirm breakouts with closing price outside the trendline, not just intraday moves, to avoid false signals.
- Early breakouts ('head fakes') are common across all triangle types; wait one to two days after initial breakout for confirmation before entering.
- Ascending triangle entry: wait for price to close above resistance with increased volume, then enter with target equal to triangle height.
- Descending triangle entry: wait for price to close below support with increased volume, confirming downside continuation.
- Set stop losses below support for ascending triangles, above resistance for descending triangles, and based on breakout direction for symmetrical triangles.
- Triangles require an existing trend to be valid continuation patterns; without prior trend, they may signal reversal instead.
- The convergence of supply line (resistance) and demand line (support) represents equilibrium that eventually resolves in direction of prevailing trend.
