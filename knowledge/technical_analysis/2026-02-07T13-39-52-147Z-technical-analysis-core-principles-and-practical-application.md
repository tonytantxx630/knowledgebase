## Key points
- Technical analysis evaluates securities using price and volume data rather than financial fundamentals
- Three core principles: market discounts everything, price moves in trends, history repeats due to market psychology
- All available information is assumed to be already reflected in current price
- Prices tend to continue established trends rather than move randomly; trend persistence is a key assumption
- Chart patterns and price movements recur because collective market psychology is consistent over time
- Common tools include moving averages, oscillators, momentum indicators, support/resistance levels, trendlines, and chart patterns
- Most widely applied in commodities and forex markets for short-term trading decisions
- Works on any security with historical trading data: stocks, derivatives, currencies, commodities
- Professional analysts often combine technical analysis with fundamental research rather than using it in isolation
- Limitations include efficient market theory challenges, inexact pattern repetition, and self-fulfilling prophecy effects when many traders use identical signals
- Historical roots trace to Charles Dow's Dow Theory in the late 1800s; CMT designation is the professional standard
- Most effective when paired with disciplined risk management and for securities driven by supply-demand dynamics
- Past performance does not guarantee future results; short-term technical signals may not predict long-term movements
