## Key points
- Gold traditionally serves as an inflation hedge, preserving purchasing power when fiat currencies devalue
- Gold functions as a safe haven asset during market crises, often inversely correlated with equities
- The gold hedge typically strengthens during periods of monetary expansion and rising inflation expectations
- Currency hedging: gold moves inversely to the US dollar, providing protection against dollar weakness
- Gold's hedging effectiveness is cyclical - it may underperform during periods of low inflation and economic stability
- Portfolio allocation: many investors maintain 5-15% gold exposure for diversification and downside protection
- Gold maintains value over long horizons but can experience extended periods of underperformance
- The gold hedge works best against systemic risks rather than isolated market corrections
- Central bank buying and selling influences gold's hedging properties significantly
- Real yields inversely drive gold prices - negative real yields historically boost gold as a hedge
