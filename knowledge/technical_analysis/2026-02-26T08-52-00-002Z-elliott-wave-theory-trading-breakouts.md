## Key points
- Elliott Wave Theory reveals repetitive wave patterns influenced by investor psychology, developed in the 1930s for understanding market cycles.
- Impulse waves follow 5-3 pattern: Waves 1, 3, 5 are motive waves; Waves 2 and 4 are corrective pullbacks.
- Wave 3 is typically the strongest and longest wave (1.618x Wave 1 length) - best for breakout trading.
- Corrective waves (A-B-C) follow impulse waves: Wave A is counter-trend, Wave B retraces toward Wave 5, Wave C completes correction.
- Key Elliott Wave rules: Wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of Wave 1; Wave 3 cannot be the shortest impulse wave; Wave 4 cannot overlap Wave 1.
- Fibonacci ratios align with Elliott Wave: Wave 2 typically 50-78.6% of Wave 1; Wave 4 typically 23.6-38.2% of Wave 3.
- Divergence between price and RSI at Wave 5 indicates potential reversal opportunity.
- Triangles (corrective patterns) precede final wave moves - breakout direction determines next trend.
- Extensions occur when one sub-wave stretches significantly - Wave 3 extension is most common.
- A sustained trend cannot terminate in three waves - look for five-wave sequences for trend continuation signals.
