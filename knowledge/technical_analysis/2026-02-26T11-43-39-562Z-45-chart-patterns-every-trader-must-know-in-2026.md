## Key points
- Chart patterns visualize collective market psychology and trader behavior, representing repeatable price structures
- Continuation patterns (bull/bear flags, triangles, cup and handle, wedges) suggest the current trend will persist after consolidation
- Reversal patterns (head and shoulders, double top/bottom, triple top/bottom) signal potential trend changes at key levels
- Support and resistance are horizontal levels where price has previously reversed; multiple tests indicate stronger levels
- Break above resistance generates bullish signals; break below support generates bearish signals
- Always wait for pattern completion before entering trades rather than anticipating the pattern
- Use volume confirmation - breakouts with high volume have higher probability of success
- Set stop-loss levels based on pattern structure - place stops beyond the pattern's boundary
- Calculate profit targets by measuring pattern height and projecting from the breakout point
- Position size should be determined by the distance to stop-loss to maintain consistent risk
- Consider overall market context - patterns work better when aligned with higher timeframe trends
- Candlestick patterns (doji, hammer, engulfing, morning/evening star) provide additional confirmation of market sentiment
- Wait for clear confirmation (close beyond pattern boundary) rather than entering on wicks or early signals
