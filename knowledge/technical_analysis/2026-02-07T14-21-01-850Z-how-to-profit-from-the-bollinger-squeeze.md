## Key points
- Bollinger Squeeze occurs when Bollinger Bands reach a six-month minimum distance apart, signaling extremely low volatility that typically precedes explosive breakouts.
- Bollinger Band Width formula: BBW = (Top Band - Bottom Band) / SMA Close. Lower BBW indicates tighter squeeze and higher probability of significant move.
- Volatility cycles predictably: equities alternate between low and high volatility periods. The squeeze identifies the transition point before explosive moves.
- Use non-collinear indicators to determine breakout direction: RSI for momentum, Intraday Intensity Index and Accumulation/Distribution Index for volume-based confirmation.
- Bullish signals: positive divergence (indicators rising while price flat/falling), volume building on up days, increasing RSI, rising accumulation/distribution.
- Bearish signals: negative divergence (indicators falling while price flat/rising), volume spikes on down days, declining RSI despite higher prices, falling accumulation/distribution.
- Band expansion confirms breakout direction: upside break causes lower band to turn down; downside break causes upper band to turn up.
- Head fakes are common after squeezes: initial move in one direction traps traders before reversing for the true breakout. Use stop-losses and be ready to reverse position.
- Never trade the squeeze in isolation. Always wait for confirmation from multiple indicators and volume analysis before entering positions.
- Volume validation is critical: genuine breakouts require volume confirmation in the direction of the move, not just price movement alone.
- Standard parameters use 20-period moving average and 2 standard deviations, but can be adjusted. Six-month lookback identifies minimum volatility levels.
- Risk management essentials: use stop-losses consistently, know exit points before entry, limit position sizes, and don't risk more than you can afford to lose.
- Price near upper band suggests overbought; near lower band suggests oversold. Use in context with other indicators, not as absolute signals.
- Dominant trends continue until equal or greater opposing force emerges. Strong uptrends may fake down before continuing higher, and vice versa.
