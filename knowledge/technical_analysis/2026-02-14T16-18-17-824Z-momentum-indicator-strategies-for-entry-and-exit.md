## Key points
- RSI signals: buy when crosses above 30 (oversold exit), sell when dips below 70 (overbought exit). Readings 40-60 distinguish trending vs sideways markets. RSI above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold.
- RSI divergences strengthen signals: price makes lower lows while RSI shows higher lows indicates weakening downward momentum (potential long entry). Extreme RSI readings above 80 or below 20 indicate stronger conditions.
- MACD bullish signal when MACD line crosses above signal line, bearish when crosses below. Histogram size indicates momentum strength - larger bars = stronger momentum, shrinking bars = weakening trend.
- MACD divergences flag reversals: if price hits new highs but MACD fails to follow, trend is losing steam. Use 12-period and 26-period EMAs with 9-period signal line as standard setup.
- Stochastic Oscillator entry when %K crosses above 20 (upward momentum), exit when dips below 80 (overbought). Works best in range-bound markets, especially when %K crosses %D near support/resistance zones.
- Momentum Oscillator: above 100 = upward momentum (entry signal), below 100 = downward momentum (exit signal). Unbounded indicator useful for trend confirmation. Bearish divergence (new price highs but oscillator doesn't follow) warns of reversal.
- Trend following strategy: use 21-period Momentum indicator on 1-hour chart. Enter when crosses above 100, exit when drops below 100. Place stop-loss just beyond recent swing highs/lows. Align with trends rather than predicting tops/bottoms.
- Bullish divergence: price forms lower low but momentum indicator (RSI/MACD) creates higher low = selling pressure weakening, potential upward reversal. Bearish divergence: price makes higher high but indicator shows lower high = fading buying momentum.
- Divergence trading requires confirmation with additional technical signals and stop-loss orders. Divergences can persist for several periods before reversing, especially in crypto with frequent false breakouts.
- Overbought/oversold strategy: RSI above 70 = consider selling or tightening stop-loss. RSI below 30 = potential buying opportunity. Prices can remain extreme for extended periods during strong trends (e.g., Bitcoin staying above 70 RSI for weeks in bull runs).
- Multi-timeframe confirmation reduces false signals: use longer-period momentum indicator on higher timeframe (e.g., 24-period on 4-hour chart) for overall trend, then shorter-period on lower timeframe (e.g., 4-period on 15-minute) for precise entries.
- Multi-timeframe rule: only trade when both timeframes align momentum direction. If 4-hour shows bullish but 15-minute shows bearish, wait for alignment. Results in fewer but more reliable trades with better risk-to-reward ratios.
- Adaptive Momentum Indicator adjusts parameters based on market volatility in real-time, filtering noise during choppy conditions. Unlike fixed-setting traditional indicators, it responds to rapid price changes or calmer periods.
- Combine momentum indicators with other technical signals and price action analysis to avoid false signals. Momentum indicators work best when confirming patterns rather than in isolation.
- Use stop-loss orders consistently with momentum strategies. Place stops just beyond recent swing highs/lows for trend-following, and adjust based on volatility for divergence/overbought-oversold plays.
