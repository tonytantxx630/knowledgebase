## Key points
- Support and resistance are horizontal price levels connecting bar highs to highs or lows to lows on a price chart
- Support/resistance forms from trading ranges (price oscillating between parallel levels) and swing points in trends (peaks in uptrends, troughs in downtrends)
- Role reversal principle: broken resistance becomes support; broken support becomes resistance
- Old peaks in uptrends act as support after breakout and retrace; old troughs in downtrends act as resistance after breakdown and retrace
- Trade support/resistance by waiting for price action entry signals (pin bars, fakey, inside bars) at key levels rather than trading the level blindly
- Key levels provide logical barriers for stop loss placement beyond the level
- Entry signals at key levels confirm potential directional move, creating high-probability setups with favorable risk/reward
- Focus on key daily chart levels only; avoid marking every minor level to prevent over-complication
- Decision framework: if a price action signal is not at a key support/resistance level, consider passing on the trade
- Price action strategies have significantly better success rates at confluent support/resistance levels
- In trending markets, look for entries as price retraces to test previous swing points where trends are most likely to resume
- Combination of key level plus price action entry signal creates highest probability scenarios: level defines risk, signal confirms direction
- Drawing levels is part skill, part art; precision improves with experience and screen time
