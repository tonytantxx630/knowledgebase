# How To Use RSI Indicator | TradingView Beginner Guide 2026

## Introduction to the RSI Indicator

The RSI indicator (Relative Strength Index) is one of the most widely used momentum oscillators in technical analysis. It compresses recent price performance into a single value between 0 and 100 to instantly judge whether momentum is heating up or cooling down.

RSI highlights overbought conditions above ~70 and oversold conditions below ~30. However, these extremes are conditions, not trade signals. A market can stay overbought during strong uptrends and oversold during violent downtrends far longer than beginners expect.

### Suggested Level Rules

- Calm markets: Use 70/30
- Strong trends: Try 80/20 to avoid fading momentum aggressively
- Ranges: 60/40 can help time mean-reversion scalps

## RSI Divergence: Spotting Reversals

### Bearish Divergence
- Price prints a higher high but RSI prints a lower high
- Hints that the rally is losing fuel

### Bullish Divergence
- Price makes a lower low but RSI makes a higher low
- Hints that sellers are exhausting

**Important**: Divergences require a trigger - structure break, failure test at resistance/support, or confirming momentum cross (e.g., MACD).

## Top RSI Combinations for Trading

### 1. RSI + EMA(50)
- Trade in direction of EMA
- In uptrends: Look for RSI dips toward 30, enter on cross back up while price respects EMA
- In downtrends: Do the opposite

### 2. RSI + MACD
- Use RSI to identify overbought/oversold context
- Require MACD line signal or histogram shift to confirm momentum turn

### 3. RSI + Volume
- If reversal is genuine, participation tends to show up
- Fading divergences on anemic volume is riskier

## Practical RSI Trading Strategy

**Bias**: Use EMA(50) on your trading timeframe. If price is above EMA, only look for longs; if below, only shorts.

**Setup (long)**:
- Wait for pullback where RSI drops below 35
- Price should be above EMA(50) for trend alignment
- Enter on RSI crossing back above 35 with bullish candle confirmation

**Risk Rule**: Stop beyond recent swing low or EMA violation

**Exit Idea**: Scale out at swing highs; final exit at RSI 65-70

## Common RSI Mistakes to Avoid

1. Treating RSI as buy-low/sell-high machine without trend filter
2. Moving stops to breakeven too soon
3. Constantly changing settings after small sample of losses
4. Ignoring higher-timeframe levels where fakeouts occur
5. Overloading charts with too many indicators

## Key Takeaways

- RSI is a versatile context tool, not a standalone entry generator
- The edge comes from consistent rules around when to act on RSI information
- Align with trend using EMA or structure
- Require confirmation (candle or MACD shift)
- Journal every trade and update playbook only after sufficient evidence
