## Key points
- Bollinger Bands consist of three lines: 20-period SMA (middle), upper band (+2 std dev), lower band (-2 std dev); bands widen during high volatility and contract during low volatility.
- Core logic: prices tend to revert to mean (SMA); touching outer bands suggests potential mean reversion opportunity but requires confirmation to avoid catching falling knives or fighting strong trends.
- Reversal setup: upper band touch/break may signal overbought condition; lower band touch/break may signal oversold; confirm with reversal candlestick patterns, RSI/MACD divergence, or volume exhaustion before entry.
- Bollinger Band Squeeze: when bands contract (low volatility), market is compressing energy for potential breakout; wait for directional breakout with strong candle close outside band.
- Breakout confirmation: look for decisive close beyond band plus higher volume to validate move; stops typically placed inside opposite band or behind consolidation range.
- Breakout targets: use nearby support/resistance levels or project the width of consolidation range in breakout direction for profit targets.
- Combine with other indicators: use volume to confirm breakout strength; RSI to validate overbought/oversold conditions; MACD crossover in breakout direction adds confluence.
- Multi-timeframe adaptation: scalpers may use shorter lookback periods (e.g., 10-15); swing traders typically stick with standard 20-period; adjust standard deviation (1.5-2.5) based on instrument volatility.
- False signal mitigation: never trade band touches in strong trends without reversal confirmation; squeeze can persist longer than expected so wait for actual breakout before positioning.
- Risk management: Bollinger Bands define volatility framework but not standalone system; always use defined stops, confirm signals with price action/volume/momentum indicators, and size positions appropriately.
