## Summary
A fundamental breakout trading principle that combines price action, volume analysis, and risk management by entering when price clears resistance with strong volume and protecting the position with a stop below the most recent swing low.

## Key points
- Valid breakouts require price to move decisively above a defined resistance level
- Rising or elevated volume confirms genuine buying interest and validates the breakout
- Stop-loss placement below the swing low protects against false breakouts
- The swing low serves as the invalidation point where the breakout thesis no longer holds
- Volume acts as a filter to distinguish between genuine breakouts and low-conviction moves

## Details
This trading principle addresses one of the most common technical setups: the resistance breakout. The key insight is that breakouts are most reliable when accompanied by increasing volume, which indicates strong participation and conviction from market participants rather than a low-volume drift through resistance.

The stop-loss placement below the swing low is critical for risk management. The swing low represents the most recent low point before the breakout attempt; if price falls back below this level, it invalidates the bullish breakout thesis and suggests either a false breakout or insufficient buying pressure to sustain the move.

This approach balances opportunity with protection. Entering on the breakout allows traders to participate early in potential trend continuations, while the swing low stop provides a logical, technically-defined exit point that limits downside risk to a quantifiable amount based on the chart structure.
