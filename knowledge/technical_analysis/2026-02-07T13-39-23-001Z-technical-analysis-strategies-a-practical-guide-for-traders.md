## Key points
- Technical analysis assumes all available information is already reflected in price, unlike fundamental analysis which examines intrinsic value
- No single strategy works in all market conditions; successful traders adapt based on trend, volatility, and market regime (range vs trend)
- Momentum strategies include RSI (trend-filtered, failure swings, divergence), MACD (breakout confirmation, pullbacks), OBV (volume precedes price), and MFI (volume-weighted momentum)
- Volatility strategies use Bollinger Bands for mean reversion in ranges and breakouts after squeezes, and Donchian Channels for trend-following breakouts
- Trend-following strategies include chart patterns (flags, pennants, head & shoulders, triangles), Ichimoku Cloud for multi-dimensional analysis, and moving average crossover systems
- Volume-based tools confirm conviction: Volume Profile identifies acceptance/rejection zones, RVOL signals institutional involvement, anchored VWAP tracks institutional cost basis
- Price action strategies focus on market structure analysis, candlestick patterns, gap fills, fair value gaps, and mean reversion without relying on indicators
- Event-driven strategies incorporate analyst rating changes, corporate insider trading activity, unusual options activity, and earnings reports into technical setups
- Volume Profile, RVOL, and VWAP help validate whether price movements have institutional backing and commitment
- Combining multiple strategies and confirmation tools improves reliability and reduces false signals
- Technical analysis strategies are probabilistic, not deterministic; risk management and discipline matter more than indicator choice
- Understanding current market regime is critical before applying any strategy; range-bound markets favor mean reversion while trending markets favor momentum
- All technical strategies require consistent execution, adaptability, and proper position sizing to be effective over time
