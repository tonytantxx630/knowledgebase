## Key points
- Confluence is the alignment of multiple technical indicators, chart patterns, or analytical tools to confirm trading decisions and filter noise
- A confluence zone is a price area where multiple technical analysis methods converge (support/resistance, moving averages, trend lines), signaling significant levels for reversals, breakouts, or continuations
- Key confluence components include indicator signals (MA for trend, RSI for overbought/oversold, MACD for momentum), chart/candlestick patterns, and support/resistance levels
- Combining support/resistance with Fibonacci levels or moving averages creates more powerful confluence zones
- Higher timeframe analysis (daily/weekly) provides broader perspective to validate lower timeframe setups
- Fundamental analysis can be integrated by aligning technical indicators with economic releases or central bank policy events
- Identify high-probability setups by looking for multiple converging factors rather than relying on single indicators
- Use multi-timeframe strategy: higher timeframe for trend direction, lower timeframe for precise entry timing
- Risk management in confluence trading: limit risk to 1-2% per trade and place stop-losses beyond key confluence levels
- Always backtest and forward test confluence strategies before live execution to validate effectiveness
