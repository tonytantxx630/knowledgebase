## Key points
- Scalping requires holding positions from seconds to minutes with high volatility - suitable only for experienced traders due to extreme speed and transaction costs
- Day trading involves intraday positions closed within the same trading session, reducing overnight risk while requiring moderate screen time
- Swing trading holds positions from days to weeks, capturing larger moves with favorable reward-to-risk ratios - ideal for both beginners and experienced traders
- Position trading spans weeks to months based on macro trends and fundamentals - requires patience and larger capital but demands least time commitment
- Moving averages (EMA, SMA) identify trend direction; use longer period averages for major trend and shorter for short-term momentum
- RSI indicates momentum and overbought/oversold conditions; values above 70 suggest overbought, below 30 suggest oversold
- MACD measures trend momentum through the relationship between two moving averages - bullish crossover signals buying opportunity
- Bollinger Bands identify volatility and breakouts; price touching upper band may signal overbought, touching lower band may signal oversold
- Multi-timeframe analysis improves trade quality: use daily/weekly charts for trend direction, hourly/15-min charts for entry timing
- Always use stop-loss orders to limit maximum loss per trade
- Position sizing should risk no more than 1-2% of account capital per trade
- Maintain minimum 1:2 reward-to-risk ratio to ensure profitability over multiple trades
- Quality over quantity in swing trading reduces overtrading and associated fees
