## Key points
- VWAP is the average price a security traded at throughout the day, weighted by volume—price changes with higher volume carry more weight than those with low volume.
- VWAP serves as both a benchmark for institutional execution quality and a dynamic support/resistance level for intraday traders.
- Price trading below VWAP suggests sellers dominate and may indicate a good entry point; price above VWAP suggests buyers are in control and potential upward continuation.
- VWAP is calculated as the cumulative total of (price × volume) for each trade divided by total volume, and resets daily at market open.
- VWAP bands (standard deviations above/below VWAP) act as potential overbought/oversold levels and dynamic support/resistance zones.
- A common trading signal: price moves below VWAP but closes above within a few bars indicates buying activity picking up, suggesting a long position with stop below the previous low.
- VWAP is most effective when combined with other indicators (RSI, moving averages) for confirmation rather than used in isolation.
- Typical intraday pattern: price often tests bands at market open, oscillates around VWAP midday, and VWAP direction at close suggests potential trend.
- VWAP provides a comprehensive view of market mood by factoring both price and volume, making it particularly useful for short-term and intraday execution.
- Key limitation: VWAP is a lagging indicator that resets daily and does not carry over to the next session, making it less useful for multi-day analysis.
- Institutions use VWAP to ensure large orders don't move price excessively and to assess whether execution was favorable relative to the day's average.
- Watch how price interacts with VWAP and bands to gauge market sentiment—the relationship between volume and price provides better indication of market mood than price alone.
