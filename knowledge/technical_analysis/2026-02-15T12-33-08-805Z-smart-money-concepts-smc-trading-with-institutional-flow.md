## Key points
- SMC premise: profit by aligning with institutional 'Smart Money' that engineers price moves to accumulate positions at optimal prices by targeting retail liquidity pools.
- Break of Structure (BOS): trend continuation signal - new Higher High in uptrend or new Lower Low in downtrend confirms trend remains intact.
- Change of Character (CHoCH): first reversal warning - failure to make new high and break below previous Higher Low (uptrend) or break above previous Lower High (downtrend).
- Liquidity pools concentrate at obvious technical levels: swing highs (buy-side), swing lows (sell-side), equal highs/lows. Institutions target these to trigger stops and fill large orders.
- Order Block (OB): last opposing candle before strong impulsive BOS move. Bullish OB = last down-candle before rally; Bearish OB = last up-candle before drop. Price returns to mitigate unfilled institutional orders.
- Breaker Block (BB): failed Order Block that becomes reversal point. Initial OB failure is often intentional liquidity grab before aggressive reversal in opposite direction.
- Fair Value Gap (FVG): three-candle formation where rapid move leaves gap (wicks of candles 1 and 3 don't overlap body of candle 2). Market returns to rebalance this inefficiency.
- SMC trading framework: (1) establish high-timeframe trend bias, (2) identify liquidity pools on lower timeframe, (3) pinpoint confluence zones (BB + FVG), (4) execute on micro-timeframe CHoCH confirmation.
- Stop hunt mechanism: institutions push price below support to trigger retail stop-losses and trap breakout sellers, providing liquidity to accumulate large long positions before rally.
- Entry execution: wait for CHoCH on micro-timeframe (e.g., 3-min) at identified POI (Breaker Block/FVG confluence), enter with stop below BB, target previous high liquidity.
- High probability setups require confluence: combining multiple SMC concepts (BB + FVG, liquidity sweep + order block mitigation) at same zone increases edge.
- Risk-reward advantage: precise POI identification allows tight stops with targets at opposite liquidity pools, often yielding 1:4+ RR ratios.
- Key limitation: highly subjective and discretionary - requires significant screen time to develop pattern recognition for 'correct' order blocks and liquidity identification.
- Active methodology: SMC is time-intensive and unsuitable for passive investing; requires focused multi-timeframe analysis and real-time chart monitoring.
