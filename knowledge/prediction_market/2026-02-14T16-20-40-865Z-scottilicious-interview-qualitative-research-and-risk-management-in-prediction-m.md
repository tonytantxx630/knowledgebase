## Key points
- Target high-probability mispriced markets (70-90 cent range): look for discrepancies where market price is 80-85 cents but fair value should be 99 cents, offering attractive risk-adjusted returns.
- Use qualitative narrative research for international elections: conduct interviews with locals from target countries (via personal network or platforms like Fiverr) to gauge consensus across demographics rather than relying solely on quantitative data or news sources.
- Cross-demographic consensus signals strong edge: when all interviewees regardless of political affiliation, age, gender, or income agree on an outcome, the prediction is safer; lack of even one supporter is a powerful signal.
- Apply human behavior psychology to trading decisions: understand that conflict is natural, people don't always follow protocol, and personality traits (like stubbornness or bipolar hesitation) often override stated intentions.
- Charisma and anti-establishment trends are durable political predictors: in the current populist era, candidates outside traditional party hierarchies with strong personal charisma tend to outperform establishment figures.
- Implement strict position sizing and hire external risk manager: to protect accumulated profits, cap maximum position size (e.g., $300-350k on high-probability bonds) and use external oversight to prevent emotional over-betting.
- Early-stage risk-on approach justified for wealth building: taking larger risks (including aped positions on already-decided UMA markets) is acceptable when building initial capital, but discipline is required after reaching targets to avoid giving back gains.
- Optimal entry timing is 4-6 weeks before resolution: prices still offer value but enough information exists to assess direction; avoid markets that create extended stress periods.
- Avoid over-applying quantitative methods to unstable environments: quant methodologies work better in stable, developed countries with honest institutions; incorporate geopolitical variables and great power influence when assessing developing nations.
- Media savvy and survival instincts override ideology: politicians like Trump prioritize self-preservation and media management over ideology, making extreme actions (like controversial pardons) predictably unlikely.
- Scale working time and capital as edge is proven: after demonstrating consistent success, increase both time allocation (50% working time) and capital deployed (borrowing against crypto holdings) to compound returns.
- Use small tracking bets to monitor developing situations: place small positions on markets you want to follow without high conviction, allowing you to stay engaged and scale up when conviction increases.
- Recognize that established politicians struggle with authenticity: voters in populist era punish flip-flopping (e.g., calling someone Hitler then working for them) and reward outsiders who sound like regular people rather than polished politicians.
