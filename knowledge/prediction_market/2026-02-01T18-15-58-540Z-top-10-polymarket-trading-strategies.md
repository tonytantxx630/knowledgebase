## Summary
A comprehensive guide to 10 data-driven prediction market strategies on Polymarket, ranging from near-risk-free arbitrage techniques to momentum and correlation plays. The strategies exploit order book inefficiencies, cross-platform pricing gaps, resolution rule ambiguities, and behavioral biases to generate positive expected value.

## Key points
- Binary complement arbitrage: Buy YES + NO shares when their combined ask price is below $1.00 for guaranteed profit at settlement
- Multi-outcome bundle arbitrage: Purchase one share of every outcome when total cost is under $1.00 to lock in risk-free gains
- Catalyst momentum trading: Capitalize on rapid repricing after breaking news by buying early repricing and selling into follow-through with mechanical exits
- Settlement-edge trading: Trade based on exact resolution criteria rather than headlines, exploiting gaps between narrative and contract rules
- Term-structure spreads: Arbitrage mispriced probability curves across same-theme contracts with different expiry dates
- Correlation hedging: Use linked markets to hedge directional risk and isolate relative value through basis reversion
- Cross-platform arbitrage: Exploit price discrepancies between Polymarket and competing platforms like Kalshi
- Favorite compounding: Grind high-probability (>90%) outcomes for steady returns while avoiding black swan tail risk
- Mention market 'No' bias: Fade overpriced YES shares in word-mention markets where retail excitement inflates probabilities
- Whale copy-trading: Monitor blockchain-transparent top traders and replicate high-conviction positions before broader market reaction

## Details
Polymarket trading requires treating the platform as a sophisticated financial exchange rather than a simple betting venue. The shift from meme coin speculation to prediction markets rewards deep knowledge, rules-based strategies, and data-driven execution over impulsive emotional trades. Real alpha comes from exploiting correlated asset lag (e.g., cabinet picks following presidential outcomes) and real-world hedging (portfolio insurance against regulatory events).

Risk-free arbitrage strategies form the foundation: binary complement arbitrage locks profit when YES ask + NO ask < $1.00, while multi-outcome bundle arbitrage guarantees returns when the sum of all outcome asks falls below the dollar payout. Both require automated scanning, precise order book depth tracking, and strict limit orders to prevent adverse selection. These techniques demand high automation and near-zero risk profiles.

Momentum and catalyst plays exploit temporary inefficiencies: catalyst momentum captures the first repricing after breaking news before all traders update, using volume spikes as confirmation and mechanical exits based on fixed probability moves or order-book imbalance flips. Settlement-edge trading converts resolution criteria into decision trees, pricing contracts based on rule triggers rather than narrative, capitalizing on the gap between headline interpretation and actual settlement logic.

Structural strategies leverage market architecture: term-structure spreads compare similar contracts across different dates to identify mispriced volatility curves, constructing hedged pairs that target spread convergence. Correlation hedging uses linked markets (e.g., Fed rate cuts vs recession odds) to isolate relative value through historical relationships and computed hedge ratios. Cross-platform arbitrage locks guaranteed profit by simultaneously buying YES on one platform and NO on another when implied probabilities diverge beyond fee thresholds.

Behavioral exploitation rounds out the toolkit: favorite compounding grinds high-probability outcomes (>90%) where implied odds exceed real-world certainty, compounding steady returns while avoiding black swans. Mention market 'No' bias fades retail excitement in word-specific markets by analyzing speaker transcript history. Whale copy-trading monitors blockchain-transparent top wallets to replicate high-conviction trades before broader market reaction, filtering for consistent performers rather than one-hit wonders.

Execution discipline is paramount: all strategies require precise spread and fee calculations, mechanical risk management, and strict sizing based on order book depth. Monitor correlation stability, define exits before entry, log rule interpretations for settlement disputes, and stress-test hedge scenarios. Success in prediction markets comes from treating each trade as a quantifiable edge with defined risk parameters, not as speculation on outcomes.
