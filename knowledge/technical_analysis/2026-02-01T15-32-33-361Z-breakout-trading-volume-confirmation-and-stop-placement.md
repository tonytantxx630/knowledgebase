## Summary
A basic breakout trading rule: enter long when price breaks above resistance on rising volume, and protect the position by placing a stop loss below the most recent swing low.

## Key points
- Price breaking above resistance is a bullish signal indicating potential trend continuation or reversal
- Rising volume during the breakout confirms genuine buying interest and reduces false breakout risk
- Stop loss placement below the swing low provides a logical exit point if the breakout fails
- This approach combines price action (resistance break) with volume analysis for higher probability setups

## Details
Resistance levels represent areas where selling pressure has previously overcome buying pressure. When price breaks above these levels, it signals that buyers have gained control and the previous ceiling may now act as support.

Volume confirmation is critical for validating breakouts. Rising volume during the breach indicates strong participation and conviction, while low-volume breakouts are more prone to failure and 'fakeouts' where price quickly reverses.

The swing low—the most recent local minimum before the breakout—serves as a natural stop loss location. If price falls back below this level, it invalidates the bullish breakout thesis, suggesting the move lacked genuine strength and the trade should be exited to limit losses.
