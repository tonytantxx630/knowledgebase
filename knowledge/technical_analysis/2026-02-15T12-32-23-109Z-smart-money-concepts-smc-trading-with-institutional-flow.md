## Key points
- Market structure forms the foundation: identify uptrends (higher highs/lows), downtrends (lower lows/highs), or sideways ranges; trends are fractal across timeframes.
- Break of Structure (BoS): price breaks above previous high (uptrend) or below previous low (downtrend), signaling trend continuation and institutional strength.
- Change of Character (CHoCH): price breaks below recent low in uptrend or above recent high in downtrend, warning of potential trend reversal and shift in market control.
- Strong vs weak levels: strong levels come from moves that broke structure (high institutional interest, reliable support/resistance); weak levels failed to break structure (lower priority).
- Supply and Demand zones: identify momentum candle (2-4x larger), mark zone from preceding candle; demand zone = buyers overwhelmed sellers (long entry), supply zone = sellers dominated (short entry).
- High-quality zones: form at strong lows/highs, create sharp moves, minimal prior interaction (fresh), align with overall trend; enter on confirmation with stops beyond zone.
- Fair Value Gaps (FVG): rapid price moves skip levels, creating inefficiency; bullish FVG = gap between preceding candle high and following candle low; price often returns to fill gaps.
- Liquidity grabs: institutions push price beyond obvious support/resistance to trigger stops, creating liquidity for their positions, then reverse direction; shows quick push, limited closing penetration, immediate reversal.
- Trading liquidity grabs: enter after price pushes beyond level but reverses within same/next candle, stop beyond extreme, target previous structure; distinguishes from genuine breakouts by quick reversal.
- Multi-timeframe framework: use highest timeframe for trend direction (weekly/daily), intermediate for trade selection (4H/1H), lower for precise entry (15m/5m); internal and external structure must align.
- Trade execution checklist: analyze market structure → identify strong levels and zones → develop if-then scenarios → wait for price at zones → enter on confirmation → manage with structure-based stops/targets.
- Zone strength decay: supply/demand zones weaken with each test; fresh zones produce strongest reactions; confluence of multiple timeframe zones increases probability.
- Risk management anchors: place stops beyond relevant structure points (not account risk alone); targets at opposing structure or 2:1+ risk-reward; scale out at intermediate levels if needed.
- Common errors: forcing trades without quality setups, entering without confirmation, improper stop placement ignoring structure, overcomplicating with indicators, abandoning system after losses.
- Learning path: master structure basics (BoS/CHoCH) on higher timeframes → add supply/demand zones → incorporate FVGs → integrate liquidity grabs; practice 50-100 simulated trades before live capital.
