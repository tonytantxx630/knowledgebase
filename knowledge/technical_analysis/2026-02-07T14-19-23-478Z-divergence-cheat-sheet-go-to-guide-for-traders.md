## Key points
- Divergence occurs when price moves in one direction but indicator (RSI, MACD, Stochastic) moves in opposite direction, signaling potential trend change
- Bullish divergence: price makes lower lows while indicator makes higher lows, suggests selling pressure weakening and potential upward reversal
- Bearish divergence: price makes higher highs while indicator makes lower highs, indicates buying momentum decreasing and potential downward reversal
- Strong divergence is most reliable: clear opposite movements between price and indicator provide highest probability reversal signals
- Medium and weak divergence show same directional movement but with less pronounced indicator swings, requiring additional confirmation before trading
- Hidden divergence signals trend continuation rather than reversal: price makes higher lows with indicator making lower lows (bullish) or price makes lower highs with indicator making higher highs (bearish)
- Exaggerated divergence involves price forming equal highs/lows (double tops/bottoms) while indicator shows divergence, strengthening reversal signal
- Always confirm divergence signals with additional technical indicators, volume analysis, or chart patterns - never trade divergence alone
- Multiple timeframe analysis improves reliability: spot divergence on higher timeframe (daily), confirm on lower timeframe (hourly) for better entries
- Hidden divergence works best in trending markets to confirm continuation, while regular divergence is more effective in ranging/sideways markets for spotting reversals
- RSI, MACD, and Stochastic Oscillator are primary tools for identifying divergence, each measuring momentum and price relationship differently
- Convergence (price and indicator moving in same direction) confirms trend strength and continuation, opposite of divergence signaling reversals
- Volume confirmation strengthens divergence signals: bullish divergence with increasing volume or bearish divergence with decreasing volume provides higher confidence
- Apply divergence trading across timeframes: 1-5 min for scalping, hourly for day trading, daily for swing trading, weekly for position trading
- Risk management with stop-loss orders is critical especially for weak and medium divergence patterns; patience is essential as divergence signals take time to develop
